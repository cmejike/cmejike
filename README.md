I'm a PhD student in Epidemiology (minor in Biostatistics) at the University of Arizona. I trained in biotechnology before epidemiology, so I tend to ask how data were generated, from sample collection to the lab, before I interpret them.

My dissertation looks at gastrointestinal outcomes of long COVID in the Arizona CoVHORT and CoVHORT-GI cohorts. I'm working on symptom and recovery trajectories, gut microbiome differences using shotgun metagenomics, and serum proteomics using SomaScan. I'm also the clinical research coordinator on CoVHORT-GI, so I've handled a lot of the samples I'll eventually analyze.

Other work I'm involved in:

- the relationship between long COVID and food insecurity (my first first-author paper, currently a preprint)
- a photovoice study on greenspace and health in Southern Arizona
- a Fred Hutch study at the Uganda Cancer Institute on delays in antibiotic treatment for neutropenic fever

I'm a BIO5 Graduate Fellow and was a Roots for Resilience Fellow with CyVerse and the Data Science Institute. I'm also a data science ambassador in my department. Before grad school, I taught DNA barcoding and PCR to secondary-school students in Nigeria, and I still enjoy explaining things more than almost anything else in research.

Most of what I write is in R (tidyverse, ggplot2, gtsummary, tidymodels), usually in Quarto. Right now I'm learning metagenomic analysis pipelines and methods for integrating multi-omic data.
